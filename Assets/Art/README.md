# Atelier Art Direction

A tactile wooden-toy workshop: maple cases with ivory inlays, teal enamel, brass hardware,
a coral lacquer ball and warm studio lighting. The low bridge uses ochre to distinguish
it from the high bridge. Meshes, surface grain, reflection lighting and the rounded UI
sprite are authored by this project; no downloaded models or stock textures are used.

## Rebuild

Save open scenes and leave Play Mode. Run **Karakuri Labo > Apply Atelier Art Direction**.
The setup updates existing shared materials and all eleven authored scenes, including the two editor-only prototypes. It is repeatable;
asset GUIDs and authored part positions remain intact. Run this after regenerating levels
or their camera controls to reapply the visual treatment.

- `Assets/Editor/AtelierGeometry.cs`: rounded boxes, thin rounded inlays, sphere bands and rings.
- `Assets/Editor/AtelierArtSetup.cs`: material palette, prop details, lighting and UI styling.
- `Assets/Editor/ToyGeometry.cs`: hollow funnel, curved track and helical brass spring.
- `Assets/Editor/ToyWorkshopSetup.cs`: five mechanical toys and their introductory stages.
- `Meshes/`: serialized reusable meshes generated by the editor.
- `Textures/`: repeatable maple grain and a rounded nine-slice UI panel.
- `Lighting/`: studio reflection cubemap and shared color grading.

Colliders, rigidbodies, joints and physical materials are preserved. The setup compares
serialized physics components before and after each prefab and scene update. Decorations
have no colliders and stay parented to their physical assembly.

Both PC and Mobile URP profiles use full resolution, 4x MSAA, soft main-light shadows and
half-resolution ambient occlusion. The first Stage 4 solution has approximately 30,000
rendered mesh triangles; measure device frame time before increasing detail further.
